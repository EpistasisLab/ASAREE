Diagnostic rules and validation scope

Minimum independent runs per condition: 3. Poisson/binomial Pearson dispersion above 3 excludes a candidate. Gaussian residuals are flagged as severely skewed when the D’Agostino–Pearson p is below 0.001 and absolute skewness exceeds 1 (at least 20 observations).

Discrete/beta/ordinal absolute parameters above 30 are flagged as boundary fits. Mean probabilities outside [1e-8, 1 − 1e-8] flag separation; NB2 alpha below 1e-6 is treated as a boundary fit. These are inherited rules pending statistical review.

Finite estimates, usable information and reproducible numerical calculations are numerical checks. They do not establish the calibration of p-values or confidence-interval coverage.

No experiment-specific simulation validation is attached to this report. Earlier validation of other reports or model families does not validate this analysis, including beta regression. Small simulation studies cannot rule out a false-positive rate of 10% without a suitable uncertainty interval and scope.

Multiplicity and interpretation

Analytic factor tests: Holm across 3 factor tests.

Resampling checks: Holm across 2 resampling p-values.

Comparisons for Approach: one comparison in its family, so the Holm-adjusted p equals the unadjusted p.

Comparisons for Setting: one comparison in its family, so the Holm-adjusted p equals the unadjusted p.

Comparisons for Approach × Setting: one comparison in its family, so the Holm-adjusted p equals the unadjusted p.

Multiplicity families are formed by model term (analytic factor tests together; resampling checks together) and by each factor or factor-pair comparison family. Unavailable p-values are omitted from Holm adjustment and receive no conclusion; this missing-test policy requires statistical review.

“Analytic result” means the analytic Holm-adjusted p-value is below 0.05; it does not use the resampling or bootstrap results.

Confidence intervals (analytic and bootstrap) are pointwise 95% intervals, not multiplicity-adjusted. Interval agreement compares whether both exclude the null value.

Separate metrics are not corrected together; cross-metric findings are exploratory. A result without clear evidence does not show that an effect is zero, and statistical evidence is not practical importance.
